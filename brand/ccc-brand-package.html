The short version

Three colours, one shape language

CCC already has a strong visual identity. It is just not showing up in the email. The website runs charcoal panels, orange section markers, heavy uppercase headings and diagonal orange corners. The outbound mail runs Times New Roman on white. This package closes that gap.

Everything here is derived from the live site rather than invented. The palette was sampled out of the logo file. The components below are the ones the site already uses, rebuilt as email safe HTML tables so they survive Outlook, Gmail and Apple Mail.

Colour

Palette

Two brand colours carry everything: CCC orange and CCC charcoal. Orange is a signal, not a surface. It marks the thing you want clicked, the section you want found, and the edge of a dark panel. When orange starts filling large areas that are not buttons, the brand starts looking like a discount flyer.

Do not

Add a second accent colour. The site has exactly one, and every sponsor logo in a CCC email brings its own. A blue or green button turns a branded send into a template someone bought.

Do

Use charcoal when you need a second surface. Depth in this brand comes from dark panels, not from more hues.

Typography

Type

The website sets headings in a condensed geometric sans. No web font renders reliably in Outlook, so email headings use a system stack at heavy weight, uppercase, with letter spacing. That reproduces the site's voice without a font that silently falls back to Times New Roman, which is exactly what happens to CCC mail today.

Do

Leave clear space equal to the height of the C mark on every side. In email that means at least 20px of padding inside the logo band.

Do not

Place the full colour logo on charcoal, or the reversed logo on white. Both leave half the mark invisible.

Do not

Put the logo on orange. The orange ring disappears into the field. If a section has to be orange, the logo goes above or below it, not inside it.

Diagonal corners on dark panels

The speaker cards on the site carry orange diagonals at opposing corners. In email this is optional decoration: it needs background images to render, so treat it as a nice to have that degrades to a plain charcoal panel in Outlook.

Voice

How a CCC email reads

The lines CCC already uses
Commercial Real Estate & Construction
Networking Reimagined
Making Business Personal

Both are on the event banner, so they are already in the room. The long line is the positioning and belongs on the site and in decks. The short one is the tagline and is the better fit for email, where it sits under the logo or closes the footer. The site's own top strip carries a third line, "a national business forum hosting private events and summit series for the commercial real estate industry," which is what the templates use as the black strip above the logo.

The audience is senior: principals, CEOs, developers, brokers with their own deal flow. They get invited to everything. The email has to answer three questions in the first screen, or it gets archived.

  • What is it. Named event, one line on who is in the room.
  • When and where. Date, time, venue. In the panel, not buried in a paragraph.
  • What do I do now. One button.

Rules

  • No em dashes. Commas, periods, or rewrite the sentence.
  • No filler openers. Never "I hope this email finds you well."
  • Name the room, not the concept. "Senior CRE executives across multifamily, industrial and hospitality" beats "industry leaders."
  • Say the ask once, plainly. "Reply yes and I will hold your seat" outperforms three paragraphs of build up.
  • Short correspondence stays under 120 words. If it needs more, it is an event email, not a note.
  • Speaker names carry title and firm on the next line, in that order, every time.
Do

Keep the confirmation emails as direct as the current ones are. The problem with the existing short sends is the packaging, not the writing. The templates preserve the plain speech and put a frame around it.

Signature

The block that closes every send

One signature, used everywhere, so the chapter list becomes a credential rather than a footer someone skims. The current version drifts: some sends say President & Founder with 13 chapters, later ones say National President & Founder with 20. Pick one and freeze it. The templates ship with the longer, more recent version.

Build rules

What breaks email, and how these files avoid it

RuleWhy
Tables for layout, never flexbox or gridOutlook renders with Word's engine. Modern CSS layout collapses to a single unstyled column.
Inline styles on every elementGmail strips most of a <style> block. Anything that has to survive is inline.
600px fixed widthThe widest column that fits every desktop preview pane without horizontal scroll.
Images always carry alt and an explicit widthRoughly half of recipients see the email with images off first. The alt text has to carry the message.
No background images for anything load bearingOutlook ignores most of them. Decoration only.
Buttons are table cells with a background colour, not styled linksA padded <a> renders as bare text in Outlook. The cell keeps the orange block.
@ encoded as &#64; in visible email addressesSome proxies rewrite raw addresses into an obfuscation script that renders as "[email protected]".
Raw canonical URLs onlyNever paste a link copied out of Gmail. Those carry a google.com/url?q= wrapper that breaks tracking and looks like phishing.
Every image on the GitHub Pages hostMail lives in inboxes forever. An image on a platform CCC does not control becomes a broken box the day that platform changes.
